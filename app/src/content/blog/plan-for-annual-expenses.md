Some of the hardest months aren't caused by surprises. They're caused by costs everyone knew were coming but nobody set money aside for. School fees at the start of a term. Uniforms and books in January. Road tax and insurance. December.

The fix is simple to describe: add up the yearly costs, divide by twelve, and set that amount aside every month. Doing it is mostly about writing the list down once.

## Make the list

Go through the year month by month and write down every cost that doesn't come monthly. Common ones in Zambia:

| Cost | When it usually comes |
| --- | --- |
| School fees | Start of each term: January, May, September |
| Uniforms, shoes, books, stationery | January, plus replacements during the year |
| University or college fees | Start of the academic year or each semester |
| Road tax, fitness and vehicle insurance | Once a year, on renewal |
| Rent deposit or advance rent | When moving, or when the landlord asks for months in advance |
| Farming inputs (seed, fertiliser) | Before the rains, around October to November |
| December and Christmas | December |
| Weddings, kitchen parties, funerals | Through the year. Estimate from last year |
| Phone, laptop or appliance replacement | Every few years. Put aside a little each year |
| Medical check-ups, glasses, dental work | Through the year |

Use last year's real figures where you have them. Bank and mobile money statements make this much easier than memory.

## Work out the monthly amount

Add up the year's total and divide by 12.

For example:

- School fees: three terms at K1,500 each, so K4,500
- January uniforms and books: K1,200
- Road tax and insurance: K2,400
- December: K1,500
- Contributions for family events: K1,200

That's K10,800 for the year, or **K900 a month**.

If K900 a month isn't possible, that's useful to know now. It means some of these costs will have to be smaller, spread out, or partly covered another way. Better to find that out in March than in January.

## Keep it separate

Put the monthly amount somewhere you won't spend it by accident: a separate bank savings account, a second wallet, or a chilimba timed to pay out before the costs fall due.

Move the money on payday, the same day every month. If you wait until the end of the month to see what's left, there usually won't be anything.

## Start mid-year without panic

If you're starting this in July and school fees are due in September, you won't have twelve months of savings by then. That's fine:

1. Work out how much you'd need for the next big cost and how many paydays you have before it.
2. Save what you can towards it.
3. If there's a gap, decide now how to cover it, whether that's a smaller cost, extra income, or a planned short-term draw you know how you'll repay.
4. From the next payday, start setting aside the full monthly amount for the year after.

The first year is the hardest. After that, the big months stop being emergencies.

## Where credit fits

If you need a small top-up to meet a deadline, a credit facility can bridge it. Draw only the gap, and know which payday will clear it. A draw you planned is very different from a draw made in a panic on the last day before fees close.

When payday is still ten days away and a cost can't wait, most salaried workers in Zambia have a few options: ask the employer for an advance, borrow from family or a friend, or use a credit product. Each one works differently, and the right choice depends on the situation.

This article compares a salary advance with a reusable credit facility like Dottra.

## How a salary advance works

A salary advance is part of your next salary paid early by your employer, then taken out of your next payslip.

- **Cost:** often free, or a small admin fee. Some employers charge nothing.
- **Speed:** depends on the employer. It can take days if it needs HR and finance approval, and some only process advances on certain dates.
- **Amount:** usually limited to a portion of your net pay.
- **Privacy:** your employer knows, and sometimes your manager too.
- **Availability:** not every employer offers one, and many limit how often you can ask.

If your employer offers advances at no cost, that's usually the cheapest option for a one-off need.

## How a reusable credit facility works

A credit facility gives you a limit you can draw from when you need to, and the amount becomes available again when you pay it back. With Dottra:

- **Cost:** a 5% processing fee per draw (minimum K1), plus a monthly charge of 20% of your average daily balance for the month. A K500 draw paid back after 15 days in a 30-day month costs K25 plus K50.
- **Speed:** a few seconds to your mobile money wallet once your limit is set.
- **Amount:** any amount from K10 up to your available credit.
- **Privacy:** your employer isn't involved. Repayments are made by you from mobile money, not deducted from your salary.
- **Availability:** it's there whenever you need it, but you need to be verified and given a limit first.

## When each one makes sense

**A salary advance is usually better when:**

- your employer offers it free or nearly free;
- you can wait for it to be approved;
- you're comfortable with your employer knowing;
- you'll need it rarely.

**A credit facility can make more sense when:**

- you need the money today, not next week;
- your employer doesn't offer advances, or you've used your allowance;
- you'd rather keep it between you and the lender;
- you want to draw a small amount and pay it back as soon as money arrives.

## Things to avoid with either one

- **Don't use either for a gap that comes back every month.** An advance this month means a smaller salary next month, and the gap gets worse. The same is true for a credit balance you never fully pay off. If the shortfall is regular, the [budget](/blog/a-monthly-budget-you-can-keep/) needs to change.
- **Don't stack them.** An advance and a credit balance due in the same month can leave you short again straight away.
- **Know the repayment date before you take either.**

## Cutting the cost of a draw

If you use Dottra for a gap before payday, the cost depends almost entirely on how long the balance is outstanding. Draw only what you need, and pay it back the day your salary arrives. A K400 draw paid back after 10 days in a 30-day month costs K20 in processing fees and about K26.67 in monthly charge.
